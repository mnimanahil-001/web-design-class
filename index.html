1. Definition & Overview

Digital literature (or electronic literature) refers to literary works created specifically to be read on digital devices, leveraging computational features such as hyperlinking, multimedia, interactive choices, and algorithmic dynamic generation.

"Electronic literature generally is considered to refer to works with important literary aspects that take advantage of the capabilities and contexts provided by the stand-alone or networked computer."

— N. Katherine Hayles, Electronic Literature Organization

2. Key Forms & Terminology

Hypertext Fiction
Literature structured around hyperlinks, allowing readers to navigate through nodes of text non-linearly.
Kinetic Poetry
Poetry that uses dynamic typography, movement, and visual pacing as essential stylistic elements.
Interactive Fiction
Narratives driven by reader decisions or command inputs, branching into multiple outcomes or paths.
